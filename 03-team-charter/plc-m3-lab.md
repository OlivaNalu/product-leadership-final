## Refine and complete your charter
- **What We Own. What this team owns.:** Our mission: Make Meridian the tool field teams choose to use for capturing, communicating, and tracking on-site issues, while automatically creating reliable enterprise project records.

What this team owns

Field adoption outcomes: We own the outcome of making the core field issue-management workflow easy, useful, and repeatable for foremen and superintendents.

The end-to-end field workflow: We own the experience of capturing an issue, communicating it, assigning ownership, tracking follow-up, and recording it in Meridian.

Field experience and usability: We own mobile-first workflow design, photo capture and annotation, reduction of unnecessary data entry, and field usability improvements.

Reliable field data: We ensure field activity produces timely, accurate, structured records that support project management, compliance, and reporting.

Evidence-led prioritization: We own the pilot, adoption metrics, workflow performance, and evidence used to determine whether the field strategy is working.

The Foundations roadmap: We own prioritization and delivery of the agreed Foundations roadmap. We protect field-adoption commitments against unplanned scope changes while remaining accountable for surfacing risks, assessing new requests, and communicating trade-offs transparently.
- **What is out of scope.:** What is explicitly out of scope

Broad enterprise analytics, reporting expansion, or general web UI refreshes that do not directly support field adoption, reliable field data, or an explicitly approved enterprise obligation.

Enterprise sales commitments, renewal negotiations, and commercial prioritization, which are owned by the Enterprise team and relevant business leaders.

Automatically absorbing additional scope because another team, customer, Sales, or senior leadership requests it.

Scaling the field experience across all customers before the core workflow has demonstrated meaningful adoption and operational value.

Accepting new work without identifying its impact on existing commitments and securing approval from the person authorized to change the relevant priorities or resource allocation.

Out of scope does not mean ignored. We assess requests, explain the trade-offs, and route them to the appropriate owner. A request may be accepted as an exception, but only through the decision process defined in this charter.
- **Cross-boundary decisions that need a joint call.:** Any decision that affects both Foundations and Enterprise priorities, shared engineering capacity, or Meridian’s enterprise reliability requires collaboration before work is committed. When a renewal-critical Enterprise request competes with engineering capacity allocated to field adoption, PM 1 and PM 2 must jointly assess customer value, contractual or renewal urgency, engineering effort, available capacity, and the impact on agreed outcomes. They must bring a recommendation and viable alternatives, including what would be delayed or deprioritized. Changes affecting shared data models, enterprise reporting, compliance, or system-of-record integrity require review by the relevant technical or functional owner and alignment between affected PMs before implementation. Changes to the agreed roadmap, quarterly Rocks, or team-level resource allocation require both PMs to present their recommendations; the Foundations initiative lead resolves competing priorities within their authority. If a request from a customer, Enterprise Sales, or senior leadership conflicts with an agreed priority, the relevant PM must promptly document the request, its urgency, the business impact, and the trade-offs for a decision by the authorized leader. Requests that require resources controlled outside Foundations, or changes beyond the initiative lead’s authority, must be escalated to the accountable product or business leader who owns those resources or commitments. No stakeholder may unilaterally redirect shared engineering capacity. Urgent risks involving binding obligations, security, compliance, data integrity, or imminent customer loss follow the immediate escalation process below. Until a decision is made, existing priorities remain in effect unless immediate protective action is needed to prevent material harm.
- **How We Decide. Who decides feature and scope calls.:** Decisions are made by the person accountable for the outcome, within agreed strategic, budgetary, technical, and resource boundaries. PM 1 owns field adoption, mobile usability, and field workflow priorities; PM 2 owns Enterprise features, reporting, and renewal-related requirements. Each PM makes decisions within their agreed roadmap and authority, but neither can independently redirect shared engineering capacity or commit another team to work. The Foundations initiative lead owns prioritization and trade-offs involving capacity allocated to Foundations, including competing Foundations and Enterprise requests, within the agreed strategy, budget, and resource allocation. Decisions involving shared architecture, data integrity, security, or compliance require review and approval from the designated technical or functional owner. The accountable product or business leader with authority over the affected priorities and resources decides changes that exceed the initiative lead’s authority, materially alter business commitments, or require capacity controlled outside Foundations. Sales and other stakeholders may raise customer needs and urgency, but they do not independently set engineering priorities. Every material decision must identify the decision owner, rationale, customer and business impact, and work being delayed, reduced, or stopped.

Roadmap and capacity changes

The agreed quarterly Rocks, roadmap, and resource allocation are the default commitments. The Foundations initiative lead may adjust sequencing or scope within the agreed strategy, budget, and capacity, provided the change does not undermine a committed outcome or materially alter an agreed business commitment. Changes to quarterly objectives, material outcome targets, or resource allocations beyond the initiative lead’s authority require approval from the accountable product or business leader. Engineering capacity is allocated against the agreed Foundations and Enterprise roadmaps at the start of each planning cycle, with the allocation and any material changes visible to the initiative lead and relevant engineering leader. Work requested by Enterprise or another stakeholder that would consume capacity committed to Foundations must be treated as a proposed trade-off, not additional work to absorb by default. Any approved change must identify what will be delayed, reduced, or stopped; the expected impact on both teams’ outcomes; the person authorizing the change; and who is responsible for communicating it. New requests are not added to the plan without an explicit decision on these trade-offs.

Decision criteria

When competing priorities arise, we assess them in this order:

Non-negotiable obligations: Binding contractual or regulatory requirements, security, compliance, and data integrity.

Strategic contribution: How directly the work advances field adoption and the core issue-management workflow, or protects a substantiated critical Enterprise commitment.

Evidence of impact: The expected customer or business outcome and the strength of the evidence supporting it.

Opportunity cost: Which agreed outcome, customer commitment, or delivery date will be affected if the work proceeds.

Reversibility: Whether the request can be tested through a small pilot or limited investment before committing significant engineering capacity.

Every material trade-off must have a named decision owner, a recorded decision and rationale, and a clear statement of what is being deprioritized.

The rule that protects the field bet

PM 1 owns the field-adoption outcome; PM 2 owns the Enterprise outcome; the Foundations initiative lead owns trade-offs involving capacity allocated to Foundations. The accountable product or business leader resolves decisions beyond the initiative lead’s authority or involving resources and commitments controlled outside Foundations. Enterprise Sales, customers, and senior leaders can raise urgent needs, but cannot independently redirect engineering capacity or override the agreed roadmap. Equally, PM 1 cannot dismiss a substantiated renewal or contractual risk simply because it falls outside the preferred roadmap. When priorities conflict, the team makes the trade-off explicit, routes the decision to the authorized owner, records the rationale, and communicates the consequences. We do not resolve competing priorities through informal commitments, repeated lobbying, or whoever escalates most forcefully.
- **How cross-team conflicts escalate.:** When a conflict arises, PM 1 and PM 2 must first try to resolve it directly within one business day, clarifying the request, customer impact, urgency, engineering effort, strategic alignment, and what would be deprioritized. Both must bring a recommendation and viable alternatives. If they cannot agree, they submit a concise decision brief to the Foundations initiative lead, who makes the decision within the next two business days if it falls within their authority. If the decision requires capacity controlled outside Foundations, exceeds the initiative lead’s authority, or materially changes a customer or business commitment, the issue is escalated to the accountable product or business leader with authority over the affected priorities and resources. That leader must make the decision or explicitly assign a decision owner and deadline within a further two business days. The person escalating is responsible for presenting the options, evidence, risks, and consequences of delay; the decision-maker is responsible for resolving the issue or assigning ownership; and the relevant PMs are responsible for communicating the outcome. If an escalation deadline is missed, the issue moves to the next level on the same business day. Decisions, rationale, and resulting priority changes must be documented and communicated to all affected teams. Conflicts must not be settled through informal commitments, repeated lobbying, or unilateral reallocation of shared engineering capacity.
- **Who resolves escalations from outside the team.:** External requests and escalations

Requests or escalations from outside Foundations—including Enterprise Sales, customers, and senior leadership—must be routed through the relevant PM and assessed before they change the roadmap or engineering commitments. The relevant PM clarifies the request, urgency, customer or commercial impact, and proposed trade-offs. The Foundations initiative lead decides whether to reprioritize work within Foundations’ agreed strategy, budget, and capacity. If the request requires resources controlled outside Foundations, changes a material customer or business commitment, or exceeds the initiative lead’s authority, the accountable product or business leader with authority over the affected priorities and resources makes the final decision. Where commercial commitments and engineering capacity are controlled by different leaders, those leaders must agree on the commitment and resource implications before work is redirected. Until approval is given, the existing roadmap and capacity allocation remain in effect, except where urgent protective action is required. Every approved change must state what work will be added, delayed, reduced, or stopped, who owns the decision, and who will communicate the outcome. No external stakeholder can bypass this process by making an informal commitment directly to engineering.

Urgent exceptions

Urgent exceptions are limited to credible risks involving binding contractual or regulatory obligations, security, compliance, data integrity, or a time-critical threat to customer retention supported by specific evidence. The person identifying the risk must immediately notify the relevant PM, the Foundations initiative lead, and the accountable technical, functional, or business leader as appropriate. The escalation must state the evidence, deadline, potential impact, and action required. The relevant technical or functional owner determines necessary protective measures for security, compliance, and data integrity risks. The accountable product or business leader decides commercial trade-offs and resource changes beyond the initiative lead’s authority. Urgency accelerates the decision process but does not automatically authorize a roadmap change or grant Sales the right to redirect engineering capacity. Affected work may be paused when necessary to prevent material harm, and any resulting change in priorities must be documented as soon as practicable.
